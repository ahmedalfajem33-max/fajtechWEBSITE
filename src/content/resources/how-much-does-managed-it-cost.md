"How much will it cost?" is the first question almost every business owner asks us. The honest answer is: it depends on what you need — but the factors that drive the price are predictable, and once you understand them you can compare quotes with confidence.

## The three common pricing models

**1. Per user, per month.** You pay a fixed monthly amount for each employee who uses technology. Everything that person uses — laptop, phone, email, Microsoft 365 and help desk support — is covered. This is the most common model today because it is simple and scales as you hire.

**2. Per device, per month.** You pay for each computer, server and network device that is managed. It can suit businesses where many people share a few machines, such as a warehouse or clinic front desk.

**3. Hourly or "break-fix".** You only pay when something goes wrong. It looks cheaper on paper, but there is no monitoring or prevention, costs are unpredictable, and your provider earns more when your systems break — the opposite of what you want.

## What affects your monthly price

- **Number of users and devices.** The biggest factor.
- **Level of support.** Business-hours support costs less than true 24/7/365 coverage with someone answering at 3 am.
- **Security included.** Endpoint detection and response, email security, security awareness training and 24/7 monitoring add real protection — and real cost. Be wary of quotes that leave them out.
- **Servers and infrastructure.** On-site servers, multiple locations and complex networks take more work than a cloud-only office.
- **Compliance needs.** Healthcare, legal and financial businesses often need extra controls and documentation.
- **On-site visits.** How often you need a technician in person, and how far away you are.

## What should be included

When you compare quotes, check whether each one includes:

- Unlimited help desk support (and during which hours)
- 24/7 monitoring, patching and maintenance
- Endpoint protection and email security
- Backup of servers *and* Microsoft 365, with tested restores
- Onboarding and offboarding of staff
- Vendor management (ISP, phones, line-of-business software)
- Regular strategy reviews and a technology roadmap

A low price that excludes security or backup is not cheaper — it moves the risk back onto you.

## Hidden costs to ask about

- Onboarding or setup fees
- Charges for after-hours or weekend work
- Project work billed separately (office moves, new servers, migrations)
- Minimum contract length and cancellation terms
- Hardware and software licences — are they included or extra?

## Managed IT vs hiring in-house

A single full-time IT employee in Calgary costs a significant salary plus benefits, training and tools — and they cannot cover nights, weekends, vacations and every specialty from networking to cybersecurity. For most businesses with fewer than 150 employees, a managed IT partner provides broader expertise and 24/7 coverage for less than one internal hire.
