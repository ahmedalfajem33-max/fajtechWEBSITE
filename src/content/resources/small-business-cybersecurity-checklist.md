Most cyberattacks on small businesses are not sophisticated. They succeed because a basic control is missing: a reused password, an unpatched laptop, a backup nobody tested. Use this checklist to find your gaps.

## 1. Accounts and passwords

- [ ] **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)** is turned on for email, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, VPN, banking and every cloud app that supports it.
- [ ] Staff use a **password manager** instead of reusing passwords.
- [ ] Former employees' accounts are **disabled on their last day**.
- [ ] Nobody uses an administrator account for everyday work.

## 2. Email

- [ ] Advanced **email filtering** blocks phishing and malicious attachments.
- [ ] **SPF, DKIM and DMARC** are configured so criminals cannot easily send email pretending to be you.
- [ ] Staff know to **verify payment or banking changes by phone** using a known number — never by replying to the email.

## 3. Devices

- [ ] Every computer has **endpoint detection and response (EDR)**, not just basic antivirus.
- [ ] Operating systems and applications **update automatically**, and someone checks that they actually did.
- [ ] Laptops are **encrypted** (BitLocker or FileVault).
- [ ] Lost or stolen phones and laptops can be **wiped remotely**.

## 4. Backups

- [ ] Servers, computers **and Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace** are backed up — cloud apps are not backed up by default.
- [ ] At least one copy is **offsite and immutable**, so ransomware cannot delete it.
- [ ] Restores are **tested regularly**. A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a plan.

## 5. Network

- [ ] A business-grade **firewall** is in place and its firmware is up to date.
- [ ] **Guest Wi-Fi is separate** from the network your business systems use.
- [ ] Remote access uses a **secure VPN or zero-trust access** with MFA — never open remote desktop to the internet.

## 6. People

- [ ] Staff receive short, regular **security awareness training** and phishing simulations.
- [ ] Everyone knows **who to call** if they click something suspicious — at any hour.

## 7. Plans and policies

- [ ] You have a simple **incident response plan**: who does what if you are hacked.
- [ ] You know your **privacy-law obligations** if personal information is exposed (see our guide to [privacy law for Alberta businesses](/resources/privacy-law-for-alberta-businesses/)).
- [ ] You have reviewed whether you need **cyber insurance** — and whether you meet its requirements (most insurers now require MFA, EDR and tested backups).
